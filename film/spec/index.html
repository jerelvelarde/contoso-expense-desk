The idea

One idea runs the picture, the music and the pacing.

Every request is a question. While the agent waits for a manager, the music waits too. Approval answers it. After it learns, the agent answers on its own.

Picture: two screens, one agent

Teams on the left, Finance's Expense Desk on the right, on one wide canvas. A thin blue line connects them and a pulse travels along it each time the agent acts. Viewers see cause and effect: a message in Teams, a step in the desk, an edit in the desk, a refreshed card in Teams.

Music: a question that resolves

Our own score. A four-note motif ends unresolved when a request comes in, holds on a suspended chord while the agent waits for the manager, and lands on the home chord when he approves. In the payoff it resolves straight away, with no wait. Nobody has to explain human-in-the-loop or learning; you hear both.

Pacing: real time, then weeks in seconds

Anything a person does plays at real speed: typing, clicking, the edit. The wait for the manager is the longest hold in the film. The learning montage compresses weeks of approvals into four beats. The payoff is fast because the agent no longer has to wait.

Type and motion

  • Headlines in the product's own Instrument Sans, 600 weight, two lines, the second line greyed. Instrument Serif appears once: the learned rule.
  • One camera on one wide canvas. Push in to whatever is moving; one pullback at the end. No whips, spins or 3D.
  • The line between the two screens is the only motion graphic. Everything else is the real UI doing its job.
  • Holds: at least 0.3 s per headline word plus 0.6 s. The wait in movement II holds about 2.5 s on Approve.
  • No browser chrome. Teams shows only the chat and the card.

Score: our own, written to the cut

QuestionFour rising notes on felt piano, ending on the second degree over a suspended chord.
WaitPulse drops out; a warm low string pad holds the suspended chord.
AnswerThe stamp lands on the downbeat; the phrase resolves home.
  • 120 BPM, bright Lydian colour resolving to major: curious and warm, never cheerful pop or thriller.
  • Marimba and mallets in an interlocking pulse, felt piano, pizzicato strings, a warm string pad. No drum kit, no synth arpeggios, no vocals.
  • Office foley tuned to the key: soft laptop keys, rubber stamps, pen ticks, paper, a glassy pulse along the line.
  • Three takes to choose from by ear, mixed to −16 LUFS. Music dips about 5 dB under typing.
